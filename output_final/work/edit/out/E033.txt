### FR193:1-54

### FR197:1-20
We find in the Quran the following verses: "They will not harm you except for some annoyance. And if they fight you, they will show you their backs. Then they will not be aided." Al-Qadi Abd al-Jabbar and exegesis of the Quran identify this as a very clear prophecy that came to pass. The Muslims that were in Medina at the time were surrounded by the Jewish tribes Bani Qaynuqa, Bani Nadir, and Bani Qurayza. And not too far away, you even had the Jews of Khaybar. Now, the verse doesn't simply predict that the Muslims will be victorious against them, but it goes as far to say that they will not be harmed in any meaningful way. Not only that, the Jews there at the time were very well equipped and were actually quite dangerous.

Al-Qadi Abd al-Jabbar also points out that revealing these verses publicly doesn't really fit in with actions of man. If a king feels negatively towards some tribes that are living on his land, the last thing that he would want to do is reveal the animosity that he feels towards them, because that would simply bring hostility towards him. Furthermore, how could he be certain that they would not be receiving harm from the Jews? One could only make a prediction like this if they had complete certainty. And in this case, that certainty came in the form of revelation.

### FR014:44-87

### FR200:1-57
This one is a prophecy from the Quran: "Oh you who believed, whoever of you should revert from his religion, Allah will bring forth in place of them a people who will love and who will love Him, who are humble toward the believers, strong against the disbelievers; they strive in the cause of Allah and do not fear the blame of a critic."

Firstly, notice that the verse says "whoever of you should revert from his religion". It says here minkum, "from among you". It doesn't say "whoever reverts from his religion" in general; it's specifically referring to the people around the Prophet, peace be upon him. Now, this is a prophecy that occurred when the Prophet, peace be upon him, passed away: a lot of the tribes in the Arabian peninsula apostated from Islam. The first part of the prophecy is that there will be people that were Muslims at the time that will leave Islam. And the second part of the prophecy is that Allah will bring Muslims to fight those that had left Islam, and that occurred when Abu Bakr fought the apostates during the Riddha wars.

Now, there are a number of ways in which this prophecy could have failed. One is that perhaps no one would have left Islam; they would have just stayed as Muslims after the Prophet passed away. It was also possible that when the apostasy actually occurred, no one would have stepped up against them. In a relatively short time, Abu Bakr successfully subjugated the apostates, and the peninsula came under the rule of Islam once again. And that together is a clear proof of prophethood.
