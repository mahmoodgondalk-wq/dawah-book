They will say: we seek refuge with Allah from you; this is our place; we will not follow you till our lord comes to us, and when our lord comes to us we will recognize him. Then Allah will come to them" — by the way, remember the reason why I said this is: this means image — "in an image that they know, and will say: I am your lord. They will say: no doubt you are our lord, and they will follow him. And then a bridge will be laid over the hellfire."

So there's two ways of explaining this. As most of you probably know, the most popular [aqidah] schools or movements right now in the Muslim world are the Ashari, [Maturidi] and Salafi movements. Now, the Ashari's, Mathuridis would have one answer to this, and the Ataris and Salafis a different one. And what they both have in common, though, is negation of Allah subhanahu wa ta'ala being physical. And here's how.

As we mentioned, Imam Tahawi's creed is generally used as a standard creed of the Muslims. When it comes to the Salafi and Athari interpretation of this hadith, this is quite simple: [adherents] of these creeds will simply say that Allah comes in whatever way befits his majesty, and that he appears in different images in whatever way befits his majesty. What they mean by this is that they confirm what is found in the text, but the [modality] of the reality of these attributes of Allah, or Allah himself, is known to Allah alone. To start describing how this coming, or jaya, of Allah is, in the sense of it being a physical movement, would be considered a bidah, a condemned innovation, in these schools.

As for the Ashares and Maturities, [adherents] of these creeds would say that the words used in hadith such as this one describe [a reality] whose meaning is consigned. Now, what do they mean by this? Well, when we say the word "to come" or the word "image", this is often not associated with something physical. So, for example, we can say "the time has come"; the same word would be used. Even though we know that the word ja'a can be used for physical coming, it is clearly not talking about something physical here, but rather the appearance of an event that was anticipated. Similarly, the scholars of these two creeds, the Ashari and the Mathuridi, would simply say that Allah subhanahu ta'ala coming to the believers has the meaning of non-physical manifestation of who he is and his attributes.

When it comes to the word surah, or image, this too can be used in a non-physical sense. So, for example, if we're talking about somebody's character, we can say "this doesn't fit your image". What we mean by this is: this does not fit your reputation, i.e. it is not coherent with your character. Or we can say something like this: "the image that you're giving off is good", which essentially means that we're saying the example you're setting for people is good. There's many more examples of how the word image can be used for non-physical reality. So when it says in the hadith that Allah would appear in one image and then in another, all this means is that Allah will make manifest his reality in different ways to the believers. And this can happen by simply, what the Asharis would say, putting an accident of awareness of Allah and his attributes in the believers. This does not mean that [Allah] changes, though, because of the fact that an observed thing is not changed by the virtue of being observed. If I was standing in a place eternally, and then somebody walked past me to look at me, if I still didn't move, I wouldn't change. So this would be the Ash ari and [Maturidi] explanation, and Allah knows best.
